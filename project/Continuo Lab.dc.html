WHY YOUR INSTINCT IS RIGHT

Balatro's antagonist is a number, not a bestiary

This is the fact that matters for a two-person team, and almost nobody says it out loud. Slay the Spire needs ~80 monsters, each with behaviour, tuning, animation and art. Balatro needs a rising integer. All of that budget moved into Jokers — three lines of code and one small illustration each. Your artist stops being a bottleneck and becomes the content engine. That single structural choice is what makes a game like this shippable by two people in eighteen months.

Separate the layer you play from the layer you build

In Slay the Spire both layers are the same object: cards. That is why it is deep and why it is heavy. Balatro splits them — you play cards, you build Jokers. The play layer stays dumb and fast; the build layer holds all the depth. It also hands your artist the personality budget: Jokers are characters, cards are furniture. Simple to play, deep to build, cheap to make. Pick this split.

Multiplicative, or it will never feel good

Additive growth is a slope; multiplicative growth is a cliff, and the cliff is the entire emotional payload. Your numbers must be able to break. Pair exponential player growth with an exponential quota and the tension curve builds itself — no encounter design, no difficulty pass, no per-fight tuning. It is the highest leverage arithmetic in the genre.
