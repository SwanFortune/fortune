01 · MY ASSUMPTIONS

You can read code and reason about systems, but you have never written a design doc. You have taste from thousands of hours of play — that is real, unusual capital. The gap is vocabulary and measurement, not instinct.

02 · STACK RECOMMENDATION

Godot 4, C#. Balatro shipped on LÖVE; Slay the Spire on libGDX. A card game is 90% UI, state machines and data — no engine wins on rendering. Godot wins on iteration speed, free licensing, and a scene tree that maps cleanly onto card/board/overlay.

Avoid web/React for a commercial run: Steam wrapping, save files and controller support all become chores. Avoid Unity unless you already know it.

03 · HOW TO USE THIS

Play three full runs before reading anything. Notice what you feel — boredom, greed, panic. Then use the tweak controls to change energy, hand size and HP, and watch which feelings die. That loop — change one number, replay, name the feeling — is the entire job.

ECHO — A 3-FIGHT PROTOTYPE

One twist on top of the Slay the Spire skeleton: every card has a tone. Play a card whose tone matches the card you played immediately before it, and its Echo clause fires. Order of play suddenly matters as much as card choice.
