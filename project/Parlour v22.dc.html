WHAT YOU ARE ACTUALLY DOING
They sit down with their composure nearly gone. You lay cards in a line and read them aloud as one sentence — as long a sentence as your energy pays for. Everything you say puts some of it back. Fill the bar before they run out of readings and they go home mended.
You lay them left to right and they stay where you put them, so plan the whole sentence before you speak any of it. Every card mends what it is worth. Order matters only where a card asks for it — some pay more for opening the sentence, some for closing it, and some only pay when the card before them carried a particular sign.
Against you: their denial is a shield: it holds off the first points of every reading, and it thickens every time they sit through one, so a reading that worked once will not work forever. Their spiral comes out of your nerve instead of theirs: every reading you give them costs you, deeper each time, and when your nerve runs out they let themselves out in whatever state you left them in. It carries from one sitter to the next — a mended sitter gives a little of it back, the back room gives more, and a new night gives all of it. Anything past full becomes faith — your score, and what the village says about you.
